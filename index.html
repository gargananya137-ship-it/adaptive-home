01
The Need

When everyday spaces become difficult.

As mobility changes, ordinary residential spaces can create friction in everyday routines. The design challenge is not to make a home feel clinical, but to remove unnecessary barriers so daily life can remain comfortable and independent.

Replace with project image
02
The User

Designed around the person.

The intended users are elderly individuals aged 60+ and people with limited mobility. The home supports capability rather than defining a person by limitation.

Safety

Reduce unnecessary risks within everyday movement and use.

Independence

Support everyday activities with less reliance on assistance.

Comfort

Create a physically and visually comfortable residential environment.

Dignity

Provide support without making the home feel institutional or hospital-like.

Accessibility

Make important spaces, fixtures, storage and circulation easier to use.

03
Design Response

From everyday barriers to deliberate design.

The project translates user needs into spatial decisions. Each response is intended to become part of the home rather than look like an added medical device.

Movement

Clear circulation

→

Wide, unobstructed movement paths support easier navigation through the home.

Risk

Fall prevention

→

Safer movement is supported through considered surfaces, edges and reduced obstacles.

Reach

Reachable storage

→

Everyday storage is considered around comfortable, practical access.

Support

Supportive furniture

→

Stable, ergonomic seating can provide support without sacrificing residential character.

Bathroom

Safer fixtures

→

Bathroom planning considers safer showering, support, circulation and comfortable fixture use.

Visibility

Lighting + contrast

→

Layered, glare-conscious lighting and visual contrast support spatial clarity and confidence.

05
Technology, Almost Invisible

Technology should support the home — not become the home.

The smartest intervention can be the one you barely notice.

Adaptive lighting

Lighting can support changing needs without becoming visually intrusive.

Voice control

Selected functions can be accessed without demanding difficult physical interaction.

Personalised settings

The environment can support individual preferences and routines.

06
Experience

What independence feels like.

The purpose of the individual interventions is ultimately experiential: making ordinary routines feel easier, calmer and more self-directed.
